How the ranking works

  • Stability is the standard deviation (SD) of log2(TPM + 1) across the selected samples. Lower values mean more stable expression. This is similar to the variation of Ct values in qPCR.
  • Only expressed genes are ranked: mean TPM ≥ 10 and TPM ≥ 2 in every selected sample, so the genes can be measured reliably by qPCR.
  • CV is the coefficient of variation of TPM. Max fold change is the ratio of the highest to the lowest value (TPM + 1).
  • DE in comparisons is how often the gene is differentially expressed (adjusted p < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| ≥ 1) in the stress-versus-control comparisons of the selected conditions. Good reference genes have 0.
  • More stable than shows the percentage of expressed genes that are less stable than the entered gene. Results: Very stable = more stable than 90% of genes and never DE; Stable = more stable than 75%; Moderate = more stable than 50%; Variable = less stable than half of the genes, or close to zero in some samples. Genes with mean TPM below 10 are marked "Low expression".
  • geNorm M is the mean variation of the expression ratio of a gene with each of the other entered genes (Vandesompele et al., 2002). Lower is better; values below 0.5 are usually considered stable.

Good practice: use at least two reference genes, choose them for the stress and tissue you study, and confirm their stability in your own samples (for example with geNorm, NormFinder or BestKeeper) before using them for normalisation.
