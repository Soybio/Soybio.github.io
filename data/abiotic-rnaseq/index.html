Abiotic stress RNA-seq expression

Gene expression of soybean in 192 public RNA-seq samples from 14 studies of salt, drought and heat stress, processed in one consistent way, with differential expression results for 30 stress-versus-control comparisons.

Overview

Raw reads of 192 samples were downloaded from NCBI SRA and processed with the same pipeline against the Williams 82 reference genome (Wm82.a4.v1). The dataset gives gene-level TPM values and read counts for every sample, and differential expression results for each stress treatment compared with its control in the same study.

Samples
192 (104 salt, 66 drought and 22 heat stress samples, including controls)
Studies
14 NCBI BioProjects
Tissues
Leaf, root, pods, and pooled leaf, root and stem
Genes
52,673 Wm82.a4.v1 genes
Comparisons
30 stress-versus-control comparisons
Responsive genes
41,973 genes are differentially expressed in at least one comparison

Processing

  • Reference: Williams 82 genome assembly 4, annotation 1 (Wm82.a4.v1); 52,673 genes.
  • Reads: reads were trimmed before alignment; the median alignment rate is 96.8%. The number of reads kept and the alignment rate of each sample are given in the sample information file.
  • Expression: gene-level read counts and TPM values were calculated for every sample.
  • Differential expression: each stress treatment was compared with the control samples of the same study and tissue. Genes with adjusted p < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| ≥ 1 are called differentially expressed (up or down).

Notes on data quality

  • Seven samples have fewer than 10 million reads after trimming: four control samples of PRJNA852689 and three samples of PRJNA641330. Their values are less precise.
  • The samples of PRJNA641330 have low alignment rates (46% to 74%; all other samples are above 80%).
  • In PRJNA285880 and PRJNA1147616, the SRA records give several time points together (for example "1, 6, 12 and 24 h"), so these samples are shown as one group.
  • The samples of PRJNA813355 are described in SRA as leaf, root and stem; they are shown as one tissue group.
